Methodology

Drops is the NFL counterpart to Whiffs, applying Tom Tango's approach to evaluating projection systems.

Drops follows four principles:

Separate playing time from rate stats

Drops evaluates playing time and rate stats separately to avoid misleading conclusions.

Playing time is measured by attempts for passers, carries for rushers, and targets for receivers. A player can appear in more than one. Other stats are evaluated as rates using the most appropriate denominator.

For example, yards and touchdowns are per completion, carry, or reception; interceptions are per attempt, and completion percentage and catch rate stand on their own. This keeps each stat in its most meaningful context.

Adjust for league context

Drops subtracts the league average from each projected rate before computing error.

Weight by opportunity

An error for a 12-carry back matters less than the same error for a 300-carry back. Systems shouldn't be penalized equally for misses on marginal and regular players.

Drops weights each error by the player's actual attempts, carries, or targets to reflect real-world impact.

Include unprojected players at league average

Limiting evaluation to players projected by every system biases toward the easiest cases. We want to reward systems that attempt harder projections.

If a player is missing from a projection system, Drops assigns them 30 attempts, 10 carries, or 8 targets for playing-time evaluation and league average for rate stats.
