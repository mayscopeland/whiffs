Methodology

Drops is the NFL counterpart to Whiffs, applying Tom Tango's approach to evaluating projection systems.

Drops follows four principles:

Score counting stats per game

Drops evaluates attempts, completions, yards, touchdowns, interceptions, carries, targets, and receptions on a per-game basis.

Players still belong to passing, rushing, and receiving pools by season opportunity (attempts, carries, or targets). Within each pool, the scored metrics are the counting stats divided by games played, so efficiency that buys more snaps shows up in the same numbers as role and durability.

Actuals, including games played, come from nflverse regular-season player stats joined on GSIS id. When a projection file omits games, Drops treats the line as a full season (16 games through 2020, 17 from 2021 on) before dividing.

Adjust for league context

Drops subtracts the league average from each projected per-game rate before computing error.

Weight by games played

An error for a one-game cup of coffee matters less than the same error for a 17-game starter. Systems shouldn't be penalized equally for misses on marginal and regular players.

Drops weights each error by the player's actual games played to reflect real-world impact.

Include unprojected players at league average

Limiting evaluation to players projected by every system biases toward the easiest cases. We want to reward systems that attempt harder projections.

If a player is missing from a projection system, Drops assigns league average for each per-game rate.
