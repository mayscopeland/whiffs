Methodology

Bricks is the NBA counterpart to Whiffs, applying Tom Tango's projection-evaluation principles to basketball.

Separate playing time from rate stats

Playing time is measured in minutes (and games). Scoring, rebounding, playmaking, and other outcomes are evaluated as rates—most often per minute, with shooting percentages and 3PA/FGA kept as their own rates.

Adjust for league context

League scoring environments change. Bricks subtracts the minutes-weighted league average from each projected and actual rate before measuring error, then reports raw, league-adjusted, and minutes-weighted league-adjusted MAE/RMSE.

Weight by minutes

An error for a 200-minute player matters less than the same error for a 2,500-minute player. Weighted league-adjusted metrics use actual minutes as weights.

Include unprojected players at league average

If a player appears in actuals but not in a projection system, Bricks assigns 500 minutes for playing-time evaluation and league average for rate stats.

Baseline

Baseline is a Marcel-like projection: last three seasons weighted 5/4/3, regression toward league average by a fixed minutes amount, a minutes prior plus aging, then scale counting stats to projected minutes.
